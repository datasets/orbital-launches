## discord

**New dataset: orbital launches and payloads per year, 1957 to 2026**

More than half of all the satellites and spacecraft ever put into orbit went up in the last five years. Of the 27,376 payloads that reached orbit from 1957 to the end of 2025, 14,722 (53.8%) were launched in 2021 to 2025. 2025 alone put 4,572 into orbit, more than the 4,493 of 1957 to 1984 combined.

The dataset counts every orbital launch attempt since Sputnik, 7,431 to 19 November 2026, with successes and failures and the launching state. 2025 was the busiest year, with 329 attempts, against 139 in 1967 at the height of the space race.

Two CSVs and the build script, aggregated from Jonathan McDowell's General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects (CC-BY 4.0): {link}

title: Orbital launch attempts and payloads per year, 1957 to 2026, from Jonathan McDowell's GCAT: 53.8% of all payloads that reached orbit by 2025 were launched in 2021 to 2025

I work on DataHub. This is a small open dataset (CC-BY 4.0) aggregated from Jonathan McDowell's General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects, the Orbital Launch Log as updated on 19 November 2026.

Two CSVs: orbital launch attempts per year with successes, failures and payloads that reached orbit (7,431 attempts since 1957, 508 of them failures), and attempts per year by launching state.

Some numbers: 14,722 of the 27,376 payloads that reached orbit from 1957 to 2025 were launched in 2021 to 2025. 2025 had 329 attempts and 4,572 payloads, more payloads than 1957 to 1984 combined (4,493).

Caveats: success and failure follow McDowell's scoring (below 0.75 is a failure); the state is GCAT's own assignment, so Electron launches from New Zealand count as NZ and Arianespace Soyuz launches count as France; near-orbital Starship tests are not counted; 2026 is partial. Payloads are satellites and spacecraft, not stages or debris. Corrections welcome.
